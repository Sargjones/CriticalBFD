Upstream spill, downstream questions: the Cambridge data centre incident and Brantford’s water Published November 11, 2026 On November 10, the Waterloo Region Record confirmed that a coolant leak at the Ascent TOR1 data centre in Cambridge released about 5,000 litres of a water-and-propylene-glycol mix on July 2. The company reported it to the province. Seventeen days later, a foul-smelling, milky-white substance was reported in West Creek (provide map and distance from TOR1) and traced through storm sewers to the site’s storm water pond. From there it reached the Speed River, a tributary of the Grand, which supplies Brantford’s drinking water. What’s reported • The Region of Waterloo, the province, the Grand River Conservation Authority (GRCA) and the City of Brantford were involved. The GRCA and Brantford were told contaminants might travel downstream and took precautionary measures. • The pond was cleaned and reopened on October 23 with ministry approval. • The operator says the spill and the blooms are unrelated and blames a stagnant pond, heat and decaying organic mater. The Region first assumed a link, then said later information showed none. • The Ministry says its samples found no contaminants of concern. What isn’t public • The Ministry’s sample results. The Record reports it couldn’t obtain them. • What Brantford’s precautions were, and what its monitoring showed. • Whether anything reached the intake. No public statement from the City about the incident has been made. What the public data shows Brantford’s July summary puts average raw river water at 6.72 NTU turbidity, against 6.29 in June and 5.65 in august, with ammonia averaging 0.11 mg/L. Nothing stands out. But monthly averages can’t show a short pulse, and the summaries don’t report dissolved oxygen, the measure a bacterial bloom would affect most. This is untreated river water, so it says nothing about treated drinking water either way. The City is the source for this. In reviewing public instruments that may fill the gap – the GRCA’s Brantford gauge records only water level and flow, and the federal archive for the same gauge doesn’t yet include 2026. What’s next Obtaining from the City of Brantford, what precautionary measures it took, and what it measured will help provide confidence to their constituents that everything that should have been done to protect the water was done. Any gaps in the City of Brantford’s ability to detect and prevent health and safety issues will paramount. Why it matters Cambridge councillors agreed in August to prepare for more data centres. The question for a city downstream isn’t whether this incident reached Brantford. It is how we would know if the next one did. Update log • November 11, 2026: Published.
